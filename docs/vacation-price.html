How to play Guess the Vacation Price

You see 8 things you might pay for on a trip, 4 from Las Vegas and 4 from Orlando: buffets, shows, park tickets, snacks and more. Drag the slider or type a number, then press Lock it in or hit Enter. The real price appears with a note, the date it was checked and a link to the source.

The closer you are, the more points you get, up to 100 per item and 800 a day. Points depend on how far off you are as a percentage, so missing a $6 item by $3 costs as much as missing a $120 item by $60. At the end you get a shopper title and a note on whether you tend to guess high or low. Play daily to grow your streak, share your colored grid, or try a bonus round.

Tips

  • Some things are free. If you think an item costs nothing, guess $0 for full points.
  • When an item says cheapest day or cheapest date, aim low. Busy days cost more, and the game uses the lowest regular price.
  • Take extra care on cheap items like snacks, rides and parking. A few dollars off is a big percentage.
  • Read your end-of-game note. If it says you guess high, nudge tomorrow's guesses down.

Fun facts

  • A pedal-powered swan boat on Orlando's Lake Eola holds up to five people.
  • When we checked, entry to the Pinball Hall of Fame in Las Vegas was free. You only pay to play, and most machines cost 25 cents to $1 a game.
  • When we checked, Nevada residents paid just $1 for a single Las Vegas Monorail ride.
