How to play Airport Code Quiz

Every bag tag shows a three-letter airport code. Your job is to work out where the bag is going. Each day brings 10 new codes from about 100 airports around the world. Pick the right city from 4 choices by clicking or pressing 1 to 4. After each answer you see the airport's full name, and often the story behind its code.

Get all 10 right to rank as an Air Traffic Controller. Eight or nine makes you a Captain, and six or seven a Frequent Flyer. Play on back-to-back days to build your streak. When you finish, try a bonus round with a random set of codes, or copy the share card, which shows a green or red square for each bag.

Tips

  • Start with the easy ones. Many codes are just the first letters of the city, like DEN for Denver, BOS for Boston and MIA for Miami.
  • If a code looks odd, think of an old name. ORD comes from Orchard Field and BNA from Berry Field Nashville.
  • Big Canadian airports have codes that start with Y, like YYZ for Toronto and YVR for Vancouver.
  • Watch for cities with two airports. New York has JFK and LGA, London has LHR and LGW, and Tokyo has HND and NRT.

Fun facts

  • The X in LAX is just filler, added when airport codes went from two letters to three.
  • Orlando's code, MCO, comes from McCoy Air Force Base, which used to be there.
  • New Orleans is MSY because the airport was built on Moisant Stock Yards.
  • Cincinnati's airport, CVG, is actually in Kentucky.
