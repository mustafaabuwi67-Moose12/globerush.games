Las Vegas and Orlando are two of the most popular vacation cities in the US, and both are built around big, bright, made-for-visitors fun. But they're very different trips. One is a desert city that barely sleeps. The other is a lake-dotted corner of Florida where the theme parks set the schedule. Here's how to choose.

Who each city suits

Las Vegas is at its best for adults: shows, restaurants, pools, nightlife and, of course, casinos. You have to be 21 to gamble in Nevada. Families do visit and there's plenty to do with kids, but much of the Strip is designed for grown-ups.

Orlando is the theme park capital. Walt Disney World, Universal Orlando and SeaWorld are all here, and Universal's Epic Universe opened in 2025. If your group includes kids, or adults who love roller coasters, Orlando is the easy pick. Getting young fans excited for the trip? Weather and when to go

Las Vegas sits about 2,000 feet up in the Mojave Desert. Summers are extreme: average July highs are well above 100°F, and the city hit 120°F for the first time in July 2024. It's very dry, with only about 4 inches of rain a year. Spring and fall are the most comfortable seasons for being outdoors. Winter days are mild and nights get cold. You can even ski at Lee Canyon, about an hour from town.

Orlando sits only about 100 feet above sea level, and its summers are hot and muggy. Afternoon thunderstorms roll through almost every summer day, usually passing within an hour or so, so plan indoor time or a break for mid-afternoon. Hurricane season runs from June through November. Being inland helps, but storms can still bring heavy wind and rain, so keep your plans flexible in late summer and fall. Park crowds tend to peak around school holidays.

What things cost

Prices change all the time, so treat these as snapshots. Each was checked in 2026 or late 2025 for our Guess the Vacation Price game.

  • A theme park day in Orlando: a 1-day, 1-park Walt Disney World ticket started at $119 before tax on the cheapest dates (October 2026), and busy days cost much more. Universal Studios Florida started at about $124 (March 2026) and Epic Universe at about $139 (December 2025). Standard parking at a Disney park was $35 a day.
  • Hotel fees in Las Vegas: many Strip resorts add a nightly resort fee on top of the room rate. Bellagio's was about $55 a night before tax (September 2026). Always compare the total price, not the headline rate.
  • Shows and attractions in Las Vegas: a daytime ride on the High Roller observation wheel started at about $23.50, and the cheapest standard ticket for The Wizard of Oz at Sphere was about $124 (both mid-2026).

Vegas also has some good free things: the Viva Vision light shows on Fremont Street's canopy, the Seven Magic Mountains art installation south of town, and the Pinball Hall of Fame, where entry is free and you only pay to play. The Fountains of Bellagio show costs nothing to watch.

The bigger difference is how costs add up. An Orlando trip is often driven by park tickets, multiplied by every person and every day. A Vegas trip is driven by the hotel, shows and food, which are easier to scale up or down.

Getting around

In Las Vegas, the airport (LAS) sits close to the south end of the Strip. Airport taxis charge flat zone fares: a ride to a Center Strip hotel was $25.25 before tip in spring 2026. Many visitors skip the rental car. The Strip is roughly four miles long, though, and walking between resorts takes longer than it looks. The Monorail runs along the east side of the Strip, and a single ride was $6.25. You'll want a car for day trips.

Orlando is spread out. Walt Disney World alone covers roughly 25,000 acres, more land than Manhattan. Disney hotel guests can use Disney's buses, monorail, boats and Skyliner gondolas, and Universal's hotels have their own shuttles and boats. If you plan to visit several resorts or head to the coast, a rental car makes life easier. Brightline trains have linked Orlando's airport with Miami since 2023, which makes a two-city Florida trip simple.

Day trips

From Las Vegas, the desert is the attraction. Red Rock Canyon is about 20 to 30 minutes from the Strip, Hoover Dam roughly 35 miles away, and Valley of Fire, Nevada's oldest state park, about an hour. The Grand Canyon Skywalk is a full day out, a few hours' drive each way.

From Orlando, the Atlantic beaches at Cocoa Beach and New Smyrna Beach are about an hour away, and so is Kennedy Space Center. On clear days you can sometimes see rocket launches from Cape Canaveral right from town. In winter, hundreds of manatees gather in the warm water at Blue Spring State Park, less than an hour's drive.

Time zones

Las Vegas is on Pacific Time and Orlando is on Eastern Time, three hours apart. Coming from the East Coast, you'll be wide awake early in Vegas. Coming from the West Coast, Orlando's park opening times can feel very early.

The short version

  • Pick Orlando if theme parks are the point, you're traveling with kids, or you want beaches and space launches nearby.
  • Pick Las Vegas for shows, food, nightlife and desert scenery, especially for an adults' getaway.
  • Skip Vegas in midsummer if you want to be outside, and plan around Orlando's afternoon storms and holiday crowds.
